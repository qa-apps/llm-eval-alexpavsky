Red-teaming in AI safety is a structured process where a team deliberately tries to make an AI system fail, produce harmful outputs, or behave in unintended ways. The goal is to find vulnerabilities before real-world users or adversaries do.

In practice, it involves:

- **Adversarial testing** – Prompting the model with tricky, edge-case, or malicious inputs to see if it produces unsafe content.
- **Stress-testing safeguards** – Checking whether safety filters, refusal behaviors, or content policies can be bypassed.
- **Scenario simulation** – Role-playing realistic misuse cases, such as attempts to extract private data, generate disinformation, or get step-by-step instructions for harmful activities.
- **Bias and fairness probing** – Testing whether the model treats different groups unfairly or produces discriminatory content.
- **Jailbreak attempts** – Trying to override the model’s guidelines using indirect phrasing, role-play, or encoded language.

The findings are then used to improve the model, update safety policies, and add better guardrails. Red-teaming is a standard part of responsible AI development and is often done by internal safety teams, external experts, or crowdsourced testers.
