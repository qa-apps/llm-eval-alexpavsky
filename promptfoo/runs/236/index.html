Red-teaming in AI safety is a structured process where people deliberately try to make an AI system behave in harmful, unsafe, or unintended ways—before real users or adversaries do. The goal is to find weaknesses, then fix them.

In practice, AI red-teaming can include:

- **Adversarial testing** – Trying to bypass safety filters, elicit disallowed content, or confuse the model.
- **Probing for harmful outputs** – Testing whether the model gives dangerous instructions, hateful content, private data, or biased responses.
- **Stress-testing edge cases** – Using unusual prompts, jailbreak attempts, role-play scenarios, or multilingual inputs.
- **Evaluating robustness** – Checking whether the model stays safe under pressure, repetition, or conflicting instructions.
- **Assessing real-world misuse** – Simulating how a bad actor might use the model for fraud, harassment, disinformation, or cyberattacks.

Red-teaming is used by AI developers, safety researchers, and sometimes external experts or crowdsourced testers. The findings help improve model training, add safety layers, update policies, and reduce risks before deployment.

A key distinction: red-teaming is done **with authorization and for defensive purposes**. It is not the same as attacking a live system without permission.
