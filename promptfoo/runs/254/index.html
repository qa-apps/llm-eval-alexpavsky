Red-teaming in AI safety is a structured process where people deliberately try to make an AI system behave in harmful, unsafe, or unintended ways—before real users or adversaries do. The goal is to find weaknesses, biases, security gaps, or failure modes so developers can fix them.

In practice, AI red-teaming can include:

- **Adversarial testing** – Trying to bypass safety filters, elicit disallowed content, or trick the model into ignoring its instructions.
- **Bias and fairness probing** – Checking whether the model produces discriminatory or unfair outputs for different groups.
- **Robustness testing** – Seeing how the model behaves under unusual inputs, edge cases, or conflicting instructions.
- **Misuse testing** – Exploring whether the model could help with harmful activities such as generating malicious code, disinformation, or phishing content.
- **Privacy testing** – Attempting to extract training data, personal information, or secrets from the model.

Red-teaming is often done by internal safety teams, external experts, or crowdsourced testers. The results are used to improve model behavior, update safety policies, and add safeguards.

If you’re interested, I can describe how red-teaming differs from penetration testing or how it fits into a broader AI safety program.
