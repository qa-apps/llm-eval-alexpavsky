Red-teaming in AI safety is a structured process where people deliberately try to make an AI system behave in harmful, unsafe, or unintended ways—before real users or adversaries do. The goal is to find weaknesses, then fix or mitigate them.

In practice, AI red-teaming can include:

- **Adversarial testing** – Trying to bypass safety filters, elicit disallowed content, or confuse the model.
- **Probing for harmful outputs** – Testing whether the model can be pushed into generating hate speech, dangerous instructions, private data, or other harmful content.
- **Jailbreaking attempts** – Using creative prompts, role-play, or indirect phrasing to get around the model’s safeguards.
- **Stress-testing policies** – Checking whether the model follows its usage policies consistently across different contexts, languages, or user personas.
- **Finding over-refusal or under-refusal** – Identifying cases where the model is either too restrictive or not restrictive enough.
- **Testing for bias or unfairness** – Looking for discriminatory outputs or uneven treatment of different groups.
- **Privacy and security checks** – Seeing whether the model leaks training data, secrets, or personal information.

Red-teaming is often done by internal safety teams, external experts, or crowdsourced testers. The results are used to improve model behavior, update safety guidelines, add filters, or retrain the model.

It is a key part of responsible AI development because it helps uncover risks that normal testing might miss.
