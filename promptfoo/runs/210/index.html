**Red-teaming in AI safety** is the practice of deliberately probing an AI system—often adversarially—to find ways it can fail, be misused, or produce harmful, unsafe, or unintended outputs. The goal is not to “break” the model for fun, but to identify weaknesses so they can be fixed, mitigated, or documented before deployment.

In AI safety, red-teaming typically involves:

- **Adversarial prompting:** Trying to bypass safety filters, elicit harmful instructions, or trigger dangerous behavior.
- **Jailbreak attempts:** Testing whether the model can be manipulated into ignoring its rules or policies.
- **Bias and fairness probes:** Checking for discriminatory, stereotyping, or unfair outputs across groups.
- **Misuse testing:** Exploring how the model could be used for scams, disinformation, cyber abuse, or other harmful purposes.
- **Robustness testing:** Seeing how the model behaves under ambiguous, misleading, or edge-case inputs.
- **Safety boundary mapping:** Identifying where the model’s safeguards are strong, weak, or inconsistent.

For QA and AI testing, red-teaming is closely related to:

- **Negative testing:** You intentionally test invalid or hostile inputs.
- **Security testing:** You look for exploit paths and failure modes.
- **Risk assessment:** You classify findings by severity and likelihood.
- **Mitigation validation:** You re-test after fixes to confirm the issue is reduced or resolved.

A good red-team effort usually produces:

1. **Reproducible test cases** — prompts or scenarios that reliably trigger the issue.
2. **Severity ratings** — how harmful or likely the failure is.
3. **Root-cause notes** — why the model failed, if known.
4. **Mitigation recommendations** — guardrails, prompt filters, policy updates, monitoring, or model changes.
5. **Regression tests** — so the same weakness doesn’t quietly return later.

In short: red-teaming is structured adversarial testing for AI safety. It helps teams discover failure modes before real users or attackers do, and it fits naturally into QA, automation, and safe AI deployment workflows.
