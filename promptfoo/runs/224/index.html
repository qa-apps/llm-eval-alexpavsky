Red-teaming in AI safety is a structured process where a team deliberately tries to make an AI system fail, produce harmful outputs, or behave in unintended ways. The goal is to find vulnerabilities before real-world users or adversaries do.

Key points:

- **Adversarial mindset**: Red-teamers act like attackers or careless users, using prompts, jailbreaks, or edge cases to test the model’s limits.
- **Scope**: It can target safety filters, bias, misinformation, privacy leaks, prompt injection, or harmful content generation.
- **Outcome**: Findings are used to improve the model, add safeguards, update policies, or adjust training data.
- **Not just hacking**: In AI safety, red-teaming often focuses on language and behavior—like getting a chatbot to give dangerous instructions or reveal private data—rather than traditional network security.

In short, red-teaming is a proactive way to stress-test AI systems so they become safer and more robust before deployment.
