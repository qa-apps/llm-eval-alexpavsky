Red-teaming in AI safety is a structured process where a team deliberately tries to make an AI system fail, produce harmful outputs, or behave in unintended ways. The goal is to find vulnerabilities before real users or malicious actors do.

In practice, it involves:

- **Adversarial testing** – Crafting prompts or inputs designed to bypass safety filters, elicit disallowed content, or trigger harmful behavior.
- **Scenario simulation** – Role-playing realistic misuse cases, such as attempts to extract private data, generate dangerous instructions, or manipulate the model.
- **Bias and fairness probing** – Testing whether the model produces discriminatory, toxic, or unfair outputs across different groups or contexts.
- **Robustness checks** – Seeing how the model behaves under edge cases, jailbreaks, prompt injections, or unusual formatting.
- **Documentation and mitigation** – Recording failures, analyzing root causes, and feeding results back into model training, fine-tuning, or policy updates.

Red-teaming is used by AI developers, safety researchers, and sometimes external experts to improve model safety before deployment. It is not about proving the system is secure, but about actively looking for ways it is not.
