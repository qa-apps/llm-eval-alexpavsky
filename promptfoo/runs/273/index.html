Red-teaming in AI safety is the practice of deliberately probing an AI system to find weaknesses, harmful behaviors, or failure modes before real users or adversaries do. It borrows the idea from cybersecurity, where a "red team" acts as an attacker to test defenses.

In the context of AI, red-teaming typically involves:

- **Adversarial testing**: Trying to make the model produce harmful, biased, misleading, or unsafe outputs.
- **Jailbreaking attempts**: Testing whether safety filters or alignment can be bypassed.
- **Stress-testing edge cases**: Exploring unusual inputs, role-play scenarios, or prompt injections.
- **Evaluating robustness**: Checking how the model behaves under distribution shift, ambiguous instructions, or conflicting goals.
- **Identifying over-refusal or under-refusal**: Finding cases where the model is either too cautious or not cautious enough.

The goal is not to break the system for its own sake, but to improve safety, reliability, and alignment before deployment. Findings from red-teaming are used to update training data, adjust system prompts, add guardrails, or revise model behavior.
