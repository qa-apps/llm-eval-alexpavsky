Red-teaming in AI safety is a structured process where people deliberately try to make an AI system behave in harmful, unsafe, or unintended ways—before real users or adversaries do. The goal is to find weaknesses, then fix them.

In practice, AI red-teaming can include:

- **Adversarial prompting** – trying to bypass safety filters or get the model to produce disallowed content.
- **Jailbreak attempts** – testing whether the model follows harmful instructions despite its guidelines.
- **Bias and fairness probing** – checking whether the model produces discriminatory or unfair outputs.
- **Privacy testing** – seeing whether the model leaks private or sensitive information.
- **Robustness testing** – evaluating how the model behaves under unusual, confusing, or malicious inputs.
- **Policy-violation testing** – checking outputs against the model’s usage policies, such as hate speech, violence, sexual content, or self-harm.

Red-teaming is usually done by internal safety teams, external experts, or crowdsourced testers. The findings are used to improve the model, update safety filters, refine system prompts, or adjust training data.

It is an important part of responsible AI development because it helps identify risks that normal testing might miss.
