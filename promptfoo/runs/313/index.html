Red-teaming in AI safety is the practice of deliberately probing an AI system to find weaknesses, vulnerabilities, or harmful behaviors before real-world users or adversaries do. It borrows the idea from cybersecurity, where a "red team" acts as an attacker to test defenses.

In the context of AI, red-teaming typically involves:

- **Adversarial testing**: Trying to make the model produce harmful, biased, misleading, or unsafe outputs.
- **Jailbreaking attempts**: Testing whether users can bypass safety guidelines or system instructions.
- **Stress-testing edge cases**: Exploring unusual, ambiguous, or high-risk prompts to see how the model responds.
- **Evaluating robustness**: Checking whether the model behaves safely across different languages, contexts, or user demographics.
- **Identifying failure modes**: Finding cases where the model confidently gives wrong information, leaks sensitive data, or follows harmful instructions.

The goal is not to break the system for fun, but to **improve safety** by discovering problems early, documenting them, and helping developers add better safeguards, training, or usage policies.
