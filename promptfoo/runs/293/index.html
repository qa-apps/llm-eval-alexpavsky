Red-teaming in AI safety is a structured process where people deliberately try to make an AI system behave in harmful, unsafe, or unintended ways—before real users or adversaries do. The goal is to find weaknesses, then fix them.

In practice, AI red-teaming can include:

- Trying to bypass safety filters or content policies
- Attempting prompt injections or jailbreaks
- Probing for biased, toxic, or discriminatory outputs
- Testing whether the model reveals private or sensitive information
- Checking if the model can be manipulated into giving dangerous instructions
- Exploring edge cases where the model becomes overconfident or misleading

Red-teaming is usually done by a mix of security experts, domain specialists, ethicists, and sometimes external testers. The findings are used to improve model behavior, update safety guidelines, add safeguards, and make the system more robust.

It is an important part of responsible AI development, alongside other practices like evaluation, monitoring, and human feedback.
