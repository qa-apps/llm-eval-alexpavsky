Red-teaming in AI safety is a structured process where people deliberately try to make an AI system behave badly—such as producing harmful, biased, false, or otherwise unsafe outputs—in order to find weaknesses before real users do.

In practice, AI red-teaming can include:

- **Adversarial testing**: Trying prompts designed to bypass safety filters or get the model to ignore its instructions.
- **Probing for harmful content**: Testing whether the model will generate hate speech, violence, instructions for illegal acts, private data, or other disallowed content.
- **Bias and fairness testing**: Checking whether the model treats people unfairly based on race, gender, religion, or other characteristics.
- **Jailbreaking attempts**: Trying to trick the model into breaking its own rules, such as by using role-play scenarios, encoding, or misleading framing.
- **Stress-testing edge cases**: Using unusual, confusing, or contradictory inputs to see if the model fails safely.

The goal is not to “break” the AI for fun, but to identify risks, measure how often failures occur, and help developers improve safety measures, training data, filters, and model behavior before deployment.

Red-teaming is often done by internal safety teams, external experts, or crowdsourced testers, and it is an important part of responsible AI development.
