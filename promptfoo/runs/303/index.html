Red-teaming in AI safety is the practice of deliberately probing an AI system to find weaknesses, failures, or harmful behaviors before real-world users or adversaries do. It borrows the idea from cybersecurity, where a "red team" acts as an attacker to test defenses.

In the context of AI, red-teaming typically involves:

- **Adversarial testing**: Trying to make the model produce harmful, biased, false, or unsafe outputs.
- **Jailbreaking attempts**: Testing whether safety filters or alignment can be bypassed.
- **Stress-testing edge cases**: Exploring unusual inputs, role-play scenarios, or prompt injections.
- **Evaluating robustness**: Checking whether the model behaves safely across different languages, contexts, or user demographics.
- **Finding over-refusals or under-refusals**: Identifying cases where the model either refuses harmless requests or complies with harmful ones.

The goal is not to break the system for its own sake, but to identify vulnerabilities so developers can improve safety, reliability, and alignment before deployment. Red-teaming is often done by internal teams, external experts, or crowdsourced testers, and it is a key part of responsible AI development.
