Red-teaming in AI safety is the practice of deliberately testing an AI system by trying to make it fail, produce harmful outputs, or behave in unintended ways—similar to how cybersecurity red teams simulate attacks to find vulnerabilities.

In the context of AI, red-teaming typically involves:

- **Adversarial testing**: Trying to bypass safety filters, elicit disallowed content, or trigger harmful behavior.
- **Stress-testing edge cases**: Probing the model with unusual, ambiguous, or malicious inputs to see how it responds.
- **Identifying failure modes**: Finding cases where the model hallucinates, leaks sensitive information, follows harmful instructions, or exhibits bias.
- **Improving safety**: Using the findings to patch vulnerabilities, refine guardrails, and improve the model's alignment with safety guidelines.

Red-teaming is used by AI developers, safety researchers, and external auditors to make models more robust and trustworthy before and after deployment.
