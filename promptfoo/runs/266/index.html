Red-teaming in AI safety is a structured process where people deliberately try to make an AI system behave in harmful, unsafe, or unintended ways—before real users or adversaries do. The goal is to find weaknesses, biases, security gaps, or failure modes so developers can fix them.

In practice, AI red-teaming can include:

- **Adversarial testing** – Trying to bypass safety filters, jailbreak the model, or elicit disallowed content.
- **Bias and fairness probing** – Checking whether the model produces discriminatory or unfair outputs for different groups.
- **Robustness testing** – Seeing how the model behaves under unusual, confusing, or malicious inputs.
- **Privacy and security checks** – Testing whether the model leaks training data, secrets, or personal information.
- **Harmful-use exploration** – Assessing whether the model could help with dangerous activities, such as creating weapons, scams, or disinformation.

Red-teaming is usually done by internal safety teams, external experts, or crowdsourced testers. The results are used to improve model safeguards, update policies, and make the system safer before deployment.
