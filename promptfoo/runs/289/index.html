Red-teaming in AI safety is the practice of deliberately probing an AI system to find its weaknesses, vulnerabilities, and failure modes—especially those that could lead to harmful, biased, unsafe, or otherwise unintended outputs. The term comes from cybersecurity, where "red teams" simulate attacks to test defenses.

In the context of AI, red-teaming typically involves:

- **Adversarial testing**: Trying to get the model to produce harmful content, reveal private data, or bypass safety filters.
- **Stress-testing edge cases**: Asking unusual, ambiguous, or contradictory questions to see how the model behaves.
- **Identifying biases**: Probing for discriminatory or unfair outputs across different demographic groups.
- **Testing robustness**: Checking whether the model can be manipulated through prompt injection, jailbreaks, or other techniques.
- **Evaluating safety mechanisms**: Assessing whether content filters, refusal behaviors, and alignment techniques work as intended.

The goal is not to break the system for its own sake, but to **find and fix problems before real users encounter them**—or before malicious actors exploit them. Red-teaming is now a standard part of responsible AI development and is used by companies, researchers, and regulators to improve model safety and reliability.
