Red-teaming in AI safety is a structured process where people deliberately try to make an AI system behave in harmful, unsafe, or unintended ways—before real users or adversaries do. The goal is to find weaknesses, then fix or mitigate them.

In practice, AI red-teaming can include:

- **Adversarial prompting** – trying to bypass safety filters or get the model to produce disallowed content.
- **Jailbreak attempts** – using creative prompts, role-play, or encoding tricks to override model guidelines.
- **Bias and fairness testing** – probing whether the model treats certain groups unfairly or produces discriminatory outputs.
- **Privacy testing** – checking whether the model leaks training data, personal information, or secrets.
- **Robustness testing** – seeing how the model behaves under unusual inputs, typos, long contexts, or conflicting instructions.
- **Harmful use-case exploration** – testing whether the model can help with dangerous activities, such as creating weapons, malware, or disinformation.

Red-teaming is usually done by internal safety teams, external experts, or crowdsourced testers. The findings are used to improve model behavior, update safety policies, add filters, or adjust training data.

It is an important part of responsible AI development because it helps identify risks that normal testing might miss.
