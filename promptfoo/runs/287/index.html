Red-teaming in AI safety is a structured process where people deliberately try to make an AI system behave in harmful, unsafe, or unintended ways—before real users or adversaries do. The goal is to find weaknesses, then fix or mitigate them.

In practice, AI red-teaming can include:

- **Adversarial prompting** – trying to bypass safety filters or get the model to produce disallowed content.
- **Jailbreak attempts** – using creative instructions, role-play, or encoding to override model guidelines.
- **Probing for harmful outputs** – testing whether the model gives dangerous advice, hateful content, private data, or misleading information.
- **Testing edge cases** – checking behavior with unusual inputs, conflicting instructions, or high-stakes scenarios.
- **Evaluating robustness** – seeing if the model stays safe under pressure, repetition, or social engineering.

Red-teaming is used by AI developers, safety researchers, and sometimes external experts to improve model behavior, update safety policies, and reduce risks before deployment. It is a defensive practice: the aim is to make the system safer, not to provide instructions for attacking it.
