For Clay’s customers, Jeff sees GPT‑Live‑1 as a way to use Clay and Codex by voice and help prospects learn about a product. Clay is working on inbound sales development features that would let an agent show a product or service to a prospect. He also sees the Decisions API as another way to integrate models into Clay and open up new use cases.

Clay’s ambition is to carry go-to-market workflows from start to finish, with agents that improve as they learn from the results. For Jeff, the goal is to empower builders “to bring the same shift that happened in software engineering to go-to-market.”

Your best engineers shouldn’t have to become managers to matter
Patrick EatonNovember 23, 2026
November 23, 2026
7 min read

Every engineering org has a ladder. Ours runs from new hire to principal engineer, and so does almost everyone else’s. It’s a good ladder because it tells engineers what’s expected at each step, and we expect most of our engineers to climb most of it.

We need to be honest about what’s at the top, though. A principal engineer is usually a very experienced, very effective engineer doing more of the same work a more junior engineer does, just bigger and with less hand-holding. The growth path from new hire to principal is linear, and companies are good at developing engineers along it.

The path into management is just as well marked. Engineers mentor someone, then lead a project, then lead a team. Somewhere along that path the job changes kind. A manager’s success stops being their own technical work and becomes their team’s: coaching, mentoring, hiring, giving hard feedback, and growing careers.

Everyone recognizes that shift, and everyone knows a great manager can change a company by driving exceptional execution or building an exceptional team. Most companies never say what the technical version of that is. So the most ambitious engineers look up, see nothing past “a bit better than last year,” and decide management is the only way to matter more.

We think the answer is a second path that changes kind, not just degree, the way management does.

AI raises the floor, not the ceiling

Coding agents changed what a normal engineering day looks like here. We have 16 engineers working across 100+ repositories. Merges across the org went from 354 in October 2025 to 1,553 in October 2026, 4.4x in a year. We wrote up how we built that system and what it changed.

Every engineer now breaks work into agent-sized tasks, runs several agents at once, and is accountable for what the agents produced. Breaking work up and delegating it used to belong to tech leads and the most senior engineers. Agents require those skills of everyone, which is why we’re rewriting our engineering ladder.

Hands-on work hasn’t disappeared. It moved. There’s less typing and more judgment, like writing a clear spec, defining correctness, and catching what the agent got wrong. The authoring agent cannot be the verifier, so the engineer still owns the end result.

All of that raised the floor. When every engineer can direct a team of agents and answer for the result, being a slightly better engineer is a thinner career story than it used to be.

Spot the bets that change the company’s direction

Agents cannot, however, decide which bets a company should make. That is what makes technical leadership a real path, and it has always mattered. It’s harder now, because technology moves faster and everyone can code, so a bet on direction carries more weight and costs more when it’s wrong.

So be precise about the job. Our most senior technical leaders are responsible for insight that changes the direction of the company: the trends, customer problems, and opportunities that change where we’re headed once somebody acts on them. This is not principal engineer plus-plus, and it is not the person who fixes the problem themselves.  They recognize what matters and direct resources at it.

For us, that has looked like:

Recognizing an entirely different solution to a product problem that changes reliability or performance by an order of magnitude, and building it

Identifying early that Model Context Protocol (MCP) would become a fundamental technology for agentic development

Defining infrastructure based on cloud technology trends that stays durable and reliable over years, not quarters
