Finding new antimicrobial peptides

Rising antimicrobial resistance is one of the most pressing global health crises of our time and could result in an estimated 10 million annual deaths globally by 2050. To prevent this, we urgently need to discover novel therapeutics. Antimicrobial peptides (AMPs), particularly bacteriocins, offer a promising alternative to traditional antibiotics because they have naturally evolved to target fundamental vulnerabilities in competing bacteria, from cell envelopes to essential intracellular machinery, making it difficult for pathogens to develop resistance.

The exponential growth of genomic and metagenomic sequencing has populated global databases with billions of natural amino acid sequences. This pool of natural data has already produced globally used bacteriocins like Nisin, alongside recent genome-mined breakthroughs such as Darobactin, Dynobactin, and Lariocidin.

Many, however, remain locked within the unannotated "dark matter" of protein databases, with their function uncharacterized because they aren’t similar to known proteins. For example, the world's leading public resource of protein sequence and functional information, UniProt, holds millions of sequences with unknown functionality, transferred directly from genome sequencing projects.

To bridge this gap, Google developed ProtNLM (Protein Natural Language Model), a deep learning model trained to directly read the underlying evolutionary “grammar” of amino acid sequences. This allows the model to predict biological function directly from raw protein sequences, enriching millions of previously unnamed UniProt entries with high-confidence functional labels.

The functional annotation scope of the system covers protein names, function comments, subcellular locations, and GO terms, in addition to keywords.

What Is the Difference: Contract Testing vs Integration Testing?

Contract testing checks that a consumer and a provider agree on the request and response format, with each side tested alone. Integration testing runs multiple components together with real dependencies. The two test types answer different questions:

A contract test asks whether the payment service sends the fields that the orders service reads.
An integration test asks whether the orders service and the payment service work together with the database in a real flow.

You need both answers before a release, and the table shows how contract testing compares to integration testing.

Aspect	Contract testing	Integration testing
Scope	The agreement of a consumer and a provider	Several components in a real flow
What runs	Each side alone, with a mock of the other side	All components together
Dependencies	A contract file replaces the other service	Real database and real services
Speed	Seconds, like a unit suite	Minutes, with a running environment

Why Build an Agentic Development Tech Stack?

Agentic workflows handle longer sequences of work throughout the delivery process.

Context can get lost
Actions become harder to trace

A well-designed stack gives you consistent control across the workflow. You can limit higher-risk actions, route decisions for approval, and verify outcomes before release.
