Chatbot answer

According to the evaluation guidance, three common pitfalls in LLM-as-a-judge frameworks are:

1. **Fluent hallucinations**: LLM output quality must be measured against task intent and evidence, not only readability. This means that the judge should evaluate the output based on its correctness and relevance to the task, rather than just its fluency.
2. **Weak retrieval**: The judge should evaluate the output's retrieval of context and information, rather than just its generation of text.
3. **Over-trusting judges**: The judge should be calibrated and validated to ensure that it is not over-trusting the output, and that it is correctly identifying errors and inaccuracies.

The pitfall that emerges when the judge evaluates its own outputs is **self-preference**. This is when the judge favors its own outputs over others, even if they are not accurate or relevant. This can occur when the judge is not properly calibrated or validated, or when it is biased towards its own outputs. To mitigate this, the guidance recommends using a different model family for the judge than the SUT, and cross-checking on the HITL anchor set.

Generation quality is measured by the same faithfulness metric applied to the final generated answer (RAGAS‑style).
